Why a group trip plan can’t survive its first surprise

Working backwards from the symptom our team kept hitting, to the five causes underneath it. The bottom cause is the one nobody’s tooling addresses — it became the project’s direction.

Symptom
The plan falls apart, and someone ends up improvising the whole trip

Planning is scattered across tools that don’t know about each other

Group chat for decisions, a spreadsheet for money, Maps for places, Notes for confirmations.

→ no single source of truth; agreed decisions get lost in the scroll

The plan is stored as a list

Day 1, Day 2, bullet, bullet. A list holds no travel time between stops and no dependency between them.

→ nothing in the data says what has to move when one thing moves

Group preferences are merged by hand, in chat

One organiser reads 200 messages and guesses the group’s consensus.

→ the organiser becomes a human merge function; quiet members get overruled

Cost only becomes visible at the end

Estimates live in someone’s head during planning; receipts arrive after the trip.

→ budget decisions are made blind, and overspend is discovered too late to fix

Then reality intervenes — and the plan is static

A flight is delayed, a venue is closed on Mondays, one person drops out. Every downstream stop has to be re-decided manually, under time pressure, on mobile data.

→ so people stop maintaining the plan and wing it — the planning work is wasted
Problem statement: CodeNection · Lifestyle Track · “Planning an Escape”. Causes 1–4 are addressed by existing apps to varying degrees; cause 5 is the gap.
